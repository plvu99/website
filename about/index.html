The moments that made me.

A few highlights from my journey that shaped how I think, work, and show up for people around me.

2017 – 2018 · Hanoi, Vietnam
Competition

Five months training from scratch — injuries, self-doubt, and a stage we were proud of.

In my freshman year, I joined a dance competition with a team I'd just met. None of us had battle experience, just a shared love for dance and a desire to challenge ourselves on one of the biggest university stages. For five months, we trained from scratch, juggling conflicting schedules, long rehearsals, and injuries, pushing through the self-doubt until we delivered performances we were genuinely proud of.

That experience laid the foundation for how I collaborate today — showing up consistently, adapting to different strengths, and finding a way through when things get hard. Resilience and courage aren't qualities I read about; they're things I earned on a dance floor.

2019 · Hanoi, Vietnam
Leadership

She left herself out. In the end, she was hugging her teammates.

As Marketing Executive for AIESEC's Global Volunteer program, I organized events to drive sign-ups for cross-cultural volunteer experiences and led an engagement campaign with 75 returnees to share their stories and inspire the next cohort. At YouthSpeak Forum, I facilitated workshops where students and business leaders discuss SDGs, getting my first real taste of how our unique perspective and words are powerful tools for change.

Facilitating the Summer Camp was harder. An 8th-grade girl in my assigned group always kept quiet and tried to leave herself out. I took any chances to engage her by asking about her day, what she had learned during the session, and what her favorite activity was. Thanks to consistent efforts, she gradually opened her heart and was more involved in the group activities and discussions, even hugged them tight sometimes. On December 31, she messaged me to say she was studying English hard to come back next year. That's when I understood what "giving back" actually feels like.

2020 – 2021 · Hanoi, Vietnam
Work

Midnight setup, last-minute errors, and 100+ people showed up anyway.

At Abivin, my four-person Marketing team was asked to organize a workshop at TECHFEST Vietnam — a national startup festival. The week before, catering budget fell short so I renegotiated the menu, printed materials came back with errors, and we couldn't test the technical setup until midnight the day before. We returned early the next morning to finalize everything.

What I learned was to break it into concrete tasks, own your piece, and keep the team aligned so you're not all reacting to every problem in silos. We delivered a smooth event for students, founders, and investors. It was the first time I felt what it's like to pull something off when the stakes are real and I've carried that operating mode ever since.

200+ Leads generated
10 Email workflows
30–57% Open rates
6–26% Click-through rates
2021 – 2022 · Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam
Work

The month I became someone the team could count on.

During the IDO launch at GemUni, I was simultaneously updating the whitepaper and pitch deck, preparing AMAs in partner communities, supporting the CEO's livestream, and reviewing social content for accuracy — all within a single month, coordinating across Product, Growth, Community, and external agencies. Last-minute changes were the norm: feature updates delayed, messaging reworked same-day, leadership requests at midnight.

When the IDO closed at $400K raised with 85K+ participants, I'd learned something more valuable than any deliverable: how to stay composed under pressure, create structure quickly, and be the person your team relies on when things get noisy. That operating mode has followed me into every high-stakes project since.

2022 – 2024 · Singapore
Work

Five countries taught me that communication is never one-size-fits-all.

At Payboy, I worked remotely with colleagues from Singapore, Indonesia, Malaysia, Pakistan, and Vietnam across Product, Marketing, and Sales. Each team had different working styles, so I adapted my communication accordingly: structured documentation for Product, quick experiment briefs for Marketing, and concise talking points for Sales. When I joined the team in Singapore for a week-long company workshop, I met in person many teammates I had previously only known through Slack and video calls; it gave me a much deeper understanding of how trust, communication, and alignment shape effective cross-functional collaboration.

That experience — adapting communication across cultures without losing the integrity of the analysis — became one of the most transferable skills I brought to USC, where I suddenly found myself in rooms with people from 15+ countries and 25+ academic backgrounds.

2024 – 2026 · Los Angeles, CA
Education

I came to learn. I stayed to make it better for the people around me.

Getting into USC Marshall's MS in Business Analytics meant leaving everything familiar to start over in a new country. I arrived in Los Angeles in a cohort of 250+ students from all over the world. Through BASA, I found a way to contribute: designing surveys to understand student needs, organizing 5 workshops for 250+ students on Tableau, Python, and machine learning, and coordinating content with alumni and industry professionals.

One of our first events was a study session, where 2nd-year students were invited to help 1st-years with homework and upcoming finals. At first, both groups were sitting separately and the 1st-years were hesitant to approach. I suggested the 2nd-years take the lead and guide the session themselves. That small shift changed the dynamic completely; it gave the 2nd-years a role worth showing up for and made it easier for 1st-years to open up.

Beyond that event, I've built friendships across MSBA, MBA, Finance, and Marketing programs through elective projects, a beach day, a bowling night, a banquet, or a quick hallway catch-up. The communities worth belonging to are the ones where you show up in different capacities.

2025 · Los Angeles, CA
Competition

Top 4 of 18 — and the most important decision wasn't in the analysis.

In the 5th Annual MSBA Global Case Competition, my team had one week to solve a complex athlete valuation challenge for Cache AI. Early discussions were scattered, so I stepped back, reframed the problem, and proposed a three-part structure to help us converge faster. Later, a conflict emerged about the presentation structure. I had a clear view on what would work better for the judges, but chose to support what would keep the team most confident under pressure.

We advanced to the final round and placed 4th out of 18 teams, presenting in front of 100+ analytics professionals, professors, managers, and the founder of Cache AI. It reinforced something I keep relearning: effective collaboration means knowing when to push for structure and when to adapt, so the team can perform its best.
